# Daily TOEIC Journey — Mastery-Gated Roadmap

## 1. How to read this roadmap

This roadmap defines the high-level journey, not a day-by-day syllabus. Curriculum planners may create as many lessons, reviews, assessment units, and remediation units as evidence requires. Phase names and outcomes provide stable planning boundaries; lesson counts and calendar duration do not.

The approximate TOEIC Listening & Reading orientations below are broad planning ranges. They are not CEFR conversions, score guarantees, or mandatory gates. Phase placement uses a profile of foundation, grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation/listening, reading, independence, and timed-test evidence. A learner may be in different working bands for different skills.

## 2. Phase overview

| Phase | Name | Approximate CEFR focus | Approximate TOEIC L&R orientation | Dominant mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | English Orientation | Zero → Pre-A1 | No meaningful score target | Learn Mode |
| 1 | Essential Foundations | Pre-A1 → A1 | Familiarization; roughly below 250 if measured | Learn Mode |
| 2 | Elementary Communication | A1 → A2 | Roughly 200–450 | Learn Mode with simple TOEIC micro-tasks |
| 3 | Functional Intermediate | A2 → B1 | Roughly 400–600 | Learn Mode plus isolated/mixed TOEIC work |
| 4 | Independent Bridge | B1 → B1+/early B2 | Roughly 550–730 | Balanced, with growing timed sets |
| 5 | Upper-Intermediate Performance | B1+ → B2 | Roughly 700–850 | Integrated Learn and TOEIC Modes |
| 6 | Advanced TOEIC Integration | B2 → B2+ | Roughly 800–930 | TOEIC Mode prominent; Learn Mode targeted |
| 7 | C1-Oriented Mastery | B2+ → approximately C1 | Often 850–990 potential, highly individual | Independent, performance-calibrated |

Ranges overlap because the constructs differ and skill profiles vary. Scores should be interpreted with test conditions, reliability, and subscores.

## 3. Progression spine

The entire course follows this developmental spine:

**orientation and sound awareness**
→ **basic sentence meaning**
→ **connected everyday language**
→ **functional workplace communication**
→ **independent multi-paragraph comprehension**
→ **complex discourse and timed performance**
→ **dense multi-source reasoning and C1-oriented autonomy**

TOEIC preparation follows a parallel spine:

**format awareness**
→ **TOEIC-like contexts without pressure**
→ **isolated part-specific micro-skills**
→ **mixed untimed sets**
→ **short timed sets**
→ **timed sections**
→ **realistic full simulations and targeted optimization**

## 4. Phase 0 — English Orientation

**Approximate range:** Zero to Pre-A1  
**TOEIC orientation:** None required; no valid score inference from beginner micro-tasks.

### Entering learner profile

The learner may not reliably recognize the Roman alphabet in English use, common sound–letter relationships, basic English instructions, word boundaries, or the idea of English subject–verb order. Grammar terminology and dictionary/IPA conventions may be unfamiliar.

### Main objectives

- Establish psychological safety, study routines, and how to use lessons, audio, transcripts, answer keys, and review queues.
- Build the concepts of letter, sound, word, phrase, and simple sentence without excessive terminology.
- Understand that English word order and grammatical endings carry meaning.
- Begin direct form–meaning links instead of permanent word-by-word translation.

### Track objectives

- **Grammar/foundations:** capitalization; basic punctuation; subject + `be`; simple subject + verb awareness; personal pronouns; singular/plural idea; `a/an` as one-instance markers; very basic statements, negatives, and yes/no questions as whole patterns.
- **Vocabulary:** essential classroom language; greetings; names; countries; numbers; days; common objects; people; core actions; basic adjectives; function words needed for instructions.
- **Listening:** detect speech versus silence, syllable/word boundaries in very short input, names, letters, digits, and learned words in slow clear speech.
- **Reading:** recognize learned words and decode very short labels, captions, and patterned sentences.
- **Pronunciation:** alphabet names where useful; basic mouth/voice awareness; selected high-impact consonant/vowel contrasts; final-sound awareness; syllable clapping; no requirement to master the full IPA.
- **TOEIC:** understand only that TOEIC has listening and reading sections; encounter optional picture/label or question/response demonstrations with no score pressure.

### Expected independence and exercise difficulty

Instructions are Vietnamese-led and strongly modeled. Exercises use one clear operation, minimal unknown language, visible examples, and immediate feedback. The learner begins choosing replay, transcript reveal, and review actions with guidance.

### Review and assessment

- Frequent same-session retrieval and next-lesson review.
- Short oral/visual recognition checks rather than long tests.
- Assessment uses demonstrated understanding of instructions, word/sentence distinction, a small durable vocabulary base, and elementary sound/print matching.
- Failure triggers alternative modeling and smaller steps, not repeated identical worksheets.

### Mastery criteria and exit capabilities

The learner can follow the recurring lesson conventions with Vietnamese support; recognize and understand a controlled bank of foundational words; read and hear very short learned sentences; identify who/what and the main action/state in simple examples; and produce or assemble a few safe patterns. Severe alphabet, sound-boundary, or instruction problems have an active remediation plan.

### Prerequisites for Phase 1

- reliable access to audio and Markdown lesson content;
- basic lesson-navigation independence;
- foundational print awareness;
- readiness to learn simple grammar patterns and larger vocabulary sets.

## 5. Phase 1 — Essential Foundations

**Approximate range:** Pre-A1 to A1  
**TOEIC orientation:** Familiarization; roughly below 250 if formally measured, with high uncertainty.

### Entering learner profile

The learner understands a small bank of memorized English and basic lesson conventions but cannot yet handle varied sentences or natural connected speech.

### Main objectives

- Build a dependable simple-sentence system.
- Understand and use high-frequency everyday language.
- Form basic questions and negatives.
- Establish pronunciation habits that protect word recognition.

### Track objectives

- **Grammar:** nouns and basic countability; singular/plural; subject and object pronouns; possessives; `be`; `there is/are`; Present Simple; basic Present Continuous; imperatives; `can`; `have/has`; articles and basic demonstratives; subject–verb agreement; basic `wh-` and yes/no questions; negation; common prepositions of place/time; basic adjectives/adverbs; coordination with `and/but/or`.
- **Vocabulary:** approximately high-frequency foundational breadth, organized by family, home, routines, food, places, transportation, weather, clothing, health basics, time, dates, quantities, common jobs, and everyday requests. Numeric targets are planning estimates, not advancement gates.
- **Listening:** learned words and phrases, simple descriptions, times/prices/dates, basic question types, short instructions, and slow mini-dialogues.
- **Reading:** patterned sentences, captions, signs, simple forms, short messages, schedules, and very short paragraphs.
- **Pronunciation:** major consonant and vowel distinctions with high functional load; final consonants; plural and third-person endings as sound and grammar; syllables; basic word stress; contractions of `be` and common auxiliaries.
- **TOEIC:** picture description language, elementary question–response intent, very simple Part 5-style form choices, and common contexts without full part simulation.

### Expected independence and exercise difficulty

The learner can complete short familiar tasks after a model. Practice moves from recognition and matching to controlled sentence building and brief independent items. Unknown vocabulary is tightly controlled.

### Review and assessment

- New high-frequency vocabulary receives dense early spacing.
- Weekly mixed checks revisit sound, meaning, spelling, sentence form, and short comprehension.
- Phase assessment uses short modules rather than one exhausting test; listening may be replayed in Learn Mode and separately sampled under controlled conditions.

### Mastery criteria and exit capabilities

The learner understands and constructs common simple statements, negatives, and questions; distinguishes basic present-time meanings; handles essential numbers and schedules; reads short functional texts; and understands carefully delivered short speech on familiar topics. Foundational concepts needed by Phase 2 are at least stable with light support, and serious gaps have been remediated.

### Prerequisites for Phase 2

Stable simple sentence order, basic verb-form awareness, core pronouns/determiners, elementary question comprehension, and a functional base of sound–word recognition.

## 6. Phase 2 — Elementary Communication

**Approximate range:** A1 to A2  
**TOEIC orientation:** Roughly 200–450.

### Entering learner profile

The learner can understand short familiar language but depends on controlled input, translation, and predictable sentence patterns.

### Main objectives

- Connect events across present, past, and future time.
- Expand daily-life language into services, travel, and introductory workplace contexts.
- Move from isolated sentences to short connected texts and exchanges.

### Track objectives

- **Grammar:** Present Simple versus Present Continuous; Past Simple; common irregular verbs; future meanings with `will`, `be going to`, and scheduled present forms at an introductory level; object/possessive pronouns; expanded determiners and quantifiers; count/noncount nouns; comparatives/superlatives; frequency and manner adverbs; `must/have to/should/may/might`; infinitive purpose and selected verb complements; basic gerunds after common verbs; introductory Present Perfect for experience/result; conjunctions for reason, result, time, and condition; basic relative clauses as comprehension-first knowledge.
- **Vocabulary:** daily services, shopping, travel, hotels, restaurants, appointments, health, basic technology, office objects, departments, jobs, routines, schedules, deliveries, customer requests, and common collocations.
- **Listening:** sentence-level tense/time cues; question intention; short exchanges; simple announcements; basic speaker role and location; connected forms of common auxiliaries; short dictation.
- **Reading:** paragraph organization; notices, messages, simple emails, advertisements, forms, menus, timetables, and basic workplace documents; scanning for names, dates, prices, and actions.
- **Pronunciation:** grammatical endings, clusters, stress in common word families, sentence focus, function-word weakening, linking between common word shapes, and recognition of contractions.
- **TOEIC:** simplified Parts 1 and 2; isolated Part 5 items based on taught grammar/vocabulary; short adapted Part 3/4 exchanges; single short Part 7 texts with generous time; introduction to distractor and paraphrase concepts.

### Expected independence and exercise difficulty

Tasks include short unfamiliar combinations of known language. The learner uses Vietnamese explanations for new structures but increasingly follows recurring English instructions. Guided practice gives way to independent short sets.

### Review and assessment

- Review mixes present/past/future, noun phrase choices, agreement, and prepositions after initial blocked teaching.
- Listening review changes speakers or contexts while retaining the target pattern.
- Phase assessment contains real-world tasks and simple TOEIC-style modules; subskill results are reported separately.

### Mastery criteria and exit capabilities

The learner can follow and produce connected basic meanings about routine, current, past, and planned events; handle common service/travel/workplace exchanges; read short functional texts; locate explicit evidence; recognize simple paraphrases; and complete adapted TOEIC items independently at an elementary level.

### Prerequisites for Phase 3

Functional control of basic tense contrasts, noun phrases, questions, modals, clause connectors, core everyday vocabulary, and short-text comprehension.

## 7. Phase 3 — Functional Intermediate

**Approximate range:** A2 to B1  
**TOEIC orientation:** Roughly 400–600.

### Entering learner profile

The learner handles predictable communication but loses accuracy or comprehension with longer sentences, less familiar wording, connected speech, and denser workplace texts.

### Main objectives

- Build reliable clause-level comprehension.
- Develop functional workplace English and independent reading/listening routines.
- Recognize common grammatical and lexical paraphrases.

### Track objectives

- **Grammar:** systematic tense/aspect consolidation; Present Perfect versus Past Simple; past and future continuous forms; basic perfect-continuous meaning where useful; modal functions and degrees of certainty; passive voice; fuller infinitive/gerund patterns; participles as adjective forms; defining/non-defining relative clauses; first and second conditionals; reported statements/questions at an introductory level; noun clauses after common reporting/mental verbs; adverb clauses; comparison and modification; common phrasal/prepositional verbs.
- **Vocabulary:** meetings, recruitment, human resources, training, purchasing, sales, marketing, banking, events, logistics, shipping, policies, facilities, maintenance, and routine business correspondence; increased collocation and word-family study.
- **Listening:** multi-turn conversations, short talks and announcements, sequence, purpose, intention, problem/solution, basic inference, paraphrase, distractor rejection, and selective note-taking.
- **Reading:** medium emails and articles; sentence structure under modification; reference words; main idea, purpose, detail, vocabulary in context, and evidence-based inference; introduction to document relationships.
- **Pronunciation:** thought groups, prominence, reductions of auxiliaries and function words, common assimilation/linking patterns, stress shifts in word families, and exposure to multiple accents at controlled speed.
- **TOEIC:** all Parts 1–7 introduced in adapted or moderate isolated form; Part 5 form/meaning choices; Part 6 local cohesion; Part 7 single passages; short Parts 3/4 sets; light time awareness without full-test pressure.

### Expected independence and exercise difficulty

The learner can select strategies, delay transcript use, locate textual evidence, and explain many answers. Mixed practice hides the target topic. Input length and paraphrase increase, while support remains available after a genuine attempt.

### Review and assessment

- Weekly reviews interleave old tense, agreement, noun phrase, preposition, clause, and lexical knowledge.
- Periodic cumulative reviews include new speakers and text types.
- Phase assessment measures untimed language competence and separately measures short timed TOEIC-style performance.

### Mastery criteria and exit capabilities

The learner comprehends the main points and important details of clear workplace conversations, talks, and multi-paragraph texts; controls core B1 grammar; recognizes frequent paraphrases; explains evidence for answers; and completes all TOEIC part types at adapted/moderate difficulty without relying on topic labels.

### Prerequisites for Phase 4

Stable clause boundaries, core tense/aspect, passive recognition, relative/adverb/noun clause foundations, B1-level functional vocabulary, and independent single-passage comprehension.

## 8. Phase 4 — Independent Bridge

**Approximate range:** B1 to B1+/early B2  
**TOEIC orientation:** Roughly 550–730.

### Entering learner profile

The learner understands clear connected English but is slowed by dense noun phrases, indirect meaning, unfamiliar paraphrase, multiple speakers, and documents that must be connected.

### Main objectives

- Transition from sentence-by-sentence processing to discourse-level comprehension.
- Increase automaticity, lexical precision, and resistance to realistic distractors.
- Begin sustained timed work without sacrificing diagnosis.

### Track objectives

- **Grammar:** perfect and progressive contrasts; passive across common tenses and with modals; relative clauses and omission; participial and infinitive modification; causatives; conditionals including third/mixed concepts at an appropriate depth; reported speech; noun-clause patterns; adverbial relationships; determiners/quantifiers at greater precision; complex comparison; introductory reduced clauses.
- **Vocabulary:** expanded business domains, contracts, finance, insurance, manufacturing, construction, real estate, healthcare, project work, performance, and policy; systematic collocations, word families, register, polysemy, and lexical paraphrase.
- **Listening:** less predictable exchanges, three-speaker tracking, speaker attitude and implied action, graphic references, natural reductions, faster details, and moderate accent variation.
- **Reading:** dense emails/articles, inserted-sentence cohesion, writer purpose/tone, inference, double passages, schedules/forms linked to messages, and faster evidence location.
- **Pronunciation:** connected-speech transformations in authentic-rate material, contrastive stress, reduced syllables, boundary detection, and perception-led shadowing.
- **TOEIC:** regular mixed sets; moderate Parts 3–7; introduction to timed clusters; distractor taxonomy; question preview and pacing experiments evaluated by evidence rather than dogma.

### Expected independence and exercise difficulty

The learner handles medium-length work with delayed feedback, keeps an error log, and chooses targeted review. Hints are removed during independent sets. Vietnamese support is selective.

### Review and assessment

- Spacing increasingly targets collocations, paraphrase families, reductions, and recurring personal errors.
- Cumulative assessment includes double-source reasoning and short timed sections.
- Readiness requires both reasonable accuracy and evidence that timing does not cause foundational collapse.

### Mastery criteria and exit capabilities

The learner can process typical workplace discourse with moderate independence, connect information across two sources, infer likely intentions, recognize multiple paraphrase types, and sustain short timed TOEIC sets. Core prerequisites remain retrievable after delay.

### Prerequisites for Phase 5

Independent B1 comprehension, emerging B2 clause and discourse control, durable high-frequency business vocabulary, and stable strategy use under moderate timing.

## 9. Phase 5 — Upper-Intermediate Performance

**Approximate range:** B1+ to B2  
**TOEIC orientation:** Roughly 700–850.

### Entering learner profile

The learner is broadly independent in familiar domains but needs greater speed, precision, inference, and control of dense or indirect language.

### Main objectives

- Reach robust B2 receptive competence across general and workplace domains.
- Automate common grammar and vocabulary decisions.
- Integrate full TOEIC part skills under increasingly realistic conditions.

### Track objectives

- **Grammar:** consolidation of complex verb phrases, modality and stance, advanced passives/causatives, complementation, reduced relatives and adverbials, participial clauses, advanced modification, inversion patterns needed for comprehension, ellipsis/substitution, and discourse connectors.
- **Vocabulary:** broad cross-domain business vocabulary plus abstract general vocabulary; nuance among near-synonyms; collocational constraints; phrasal and prepositional verbs; derivational morphology; common idiomatic but test-appropriate expressions.
- **Listening:** authentic-rate conversations/talks, embedded details, indirect requests, attitude, multi-step intent, paraphrase chains, graphic integration, and longer retention spans.
- **Reading:** longer articles and document sets, dense noun phrases, cohesion across paragraphs, triple-passage introduction, cross-document contradictions/updates, tone, and efficient scanning/skimming choices.
- **Pronunciation:** rapid connected-speech parsing, accent accommodation, focus and implication, reductions that obscure function words, and targeted perception repair.
- **TOEIC:** realistic item structures in all parts; timed part clusters and selected sections; accuracy–speed tradeoff measurement; systematic trap review; first full simulations when stamina and coverage are ready.

### Expected independence and exercise difficulty

The learner works primarily without hints during performance tasks, justifies answers with evidence, and initiates remediation from state reports. English explanations become common, with Vietnamese retained for precision.

### Review and assessment

- Reviews sample across long intervals and different contexts.
- Assessments separate knowledge limitations, processing speed, listening perception, and strategy mistakes.
- At least two varied evidence points are required before declaring a high-stakes prerequisite durable.

### Mastery criteria and exit capabilities

The learner understands complex main ideas and most important details in standard workplace/general input, reasons across multiple texts, handles indirect and paraphrased meaning, and performs consistently on realistic TOEIC sections. Timing weaknesses are localized rather than global.

### Prerequisites for Phase 6

Broad B2 readiness, strong automatic control of core grammar/lexis, resilient listening at near-test speed, multi-document reading, and sufficient stamina for section-length work.

## 10. Phase 6 — Advanced TOEIC Integration

**Approximate range:** B2 to B2+  
**TOEIC orientation:** Roughly 800–930.

### Entering learner profile

The learner has strong general comprehension and test familiarity but still loses points through nuance, rare-but-relevant structures, dense paraphrase, subtle inference, processing speed, attention, or personal error patterns.

### Main objectives

- Convert B2 competence into consistent high-level TOEIC performance.
- Diagnose and remove individual bottlenecks.
- Extend language beyond the test so improvement does not plateau at format familiarity.

### Track objectives

- **Grammar:** precision and ambiguity resolution; long-distance agreement/reference; compressed modification; advanced clause reduction; modality, condition, concession, and stance; formal written patterns; error-prone exceptions selected by frequency and value.
- **Vocabulary:** advanced business/general vocabulary, nuanced collocations, polysemy, register, nominalization, idiomatic paraphrase, and domain transfer; targeted low-frequency items only when coverage justifies them.
- **Listening:** high-speed natural delivery, speaker switching, subtle intent, distractor timing, implied next actions, dense details, and robust accent adaptation.
- **Reading:** rapid structural parsing, long dense documents, triple passages, cross-source chronology and reference, implicit purpose, and efficient evidence verification.
- **Pronunciation:** individualized perception work based on errors; weak-form and boundary ambiguity; prosody as evidence for stance and contrast.
- **TOEIC:** timed sections and full simulations under standardized conditions; pacing plans by part; confidence calibration; targeted item families based on data; endurance and recovery routines.

### Expected independence and exercise difficulty

The learner manages study plans, chooses analysis depth, and uses English-first explanations. Learn Mode is reactivated for genuine knowledge gaps. Difficulty comes from density, speed, implication, and integration—not gratuitously obscure words.

### Review and assessment

- Personal error patterns drive a substantial share of review.
- Parallel-form assessments check whether gains transfer beyond memorized items.
- Rolling results track accuracy, time, confidence, error type, and durability.
- Mock scores are reported with conditions and normal variation, never as guarantees.

### Mastery criteria and exit capabilities

The learner performs strongly and consistently across all TOEIC parts, identifies root causes of misses, maintains accuracy under realistic timing, and comprehends varied B2+ workplace/general input. Remaining weaknesses are narrow enough for advanced individualized work.

### Prerequisites for Phase 7

Durable B2 competence, independent remediation habits, stable high-level TOEIC performance across more than one form, and readiness for broader C1-oriented complexity.

## 11. Phase 7 — C1-Oriented Mastery

**Approximate range:** B2+ to approximately C1  
**TOEIC orientation:** Often 850–990 potential, with ceiling effects and individual variation.

### Entering learner profile

The learner already handles most TOEIC material and broad B2 input. Growth now depends on nuance, breadth, speed, complex discourse, precise inference, and sustained autonomous exposure.

### Main objectives

- Develop flexible C1-oriented receptive competence beyond TOEIC's ceiling.
- Maintain or optimize high TOEIC performance without narrowing the curriculum.
- Build long-term autonomous learning and transfer to unfamiliar domains.

### Track objectives

- **Grammar/discourse:** nuanced modality and stance; information structure; cohesion and coherence; rhetorical relationships; ellipsis, substitution, inversion, emphasis, and compressed formal style; ambiguity and authorial choice.
- **Vocabulary:** broad advanced general, professional, and selected academic lexis; connotation, register, semantic prosody, metaphorical extension, discipline-neutral academic vocabulary, and sophisticated paraphrase networks.
- **Listening:** fast, information-dense multi-speaker input; implicit agreement/disagreement; humor or idiomatic nuance when relevant; unfamiliar but comprehensible topics; robust note selection and synthesis.
- **Reading:** complex long-form articles and professional documents; rhetorical purpose; subtle tone; synthesis, evaluation, and cross-document reasoning with partially implicit links.
- **Pronunciation:** perception across broader accents and speaking styles; prosodic nuance; maintenance of intelligibility if productive practice is used.
- **TOEIC:** precision at the ceiling, efficient verification, rare error analysis, periodic full simulations, and maintenance rather than endless repetition of easy format drills.

### Expected independence and exercise difficulty

The learner is largely autonomous, uses English directly, evaluates sources and strategies, and requests or generates targeted remediation responsibly. Tasks may be long and cognitively dense but remain relevant and answerable.

### Review and assessment

- Long-interval retrieval and transfer tasks replace dense routine repetition for stable basics.
- Assessment combines TOEIC simulations with broader C1-oriented listening/reading and self-explanation.
- A high TOEIC result alone does not prove C1; broader evidence is required.

### Mastery criteria and exit capabilities

The learner can understand demanding extended input, follow implicit relationships, synthesize multiple sources, interpret nuance and stance, and sustain accurate high-speed performance. The learner can independently identify gaps, plan review, and continue developing. Course completion signifies achievement of the defined profile, not an endpoint to English learning.

### Post-course continuation

Maintain broad reading/listening, periodic vocabulary and error review, authentic professional use, and occasional standardized practice if a score must be retained or renewed.

## 12. TOEIC Parts 1–7 progression

| Part | Foundation before format | First controlled use | Intermediate development | Advanced performance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Part 1 Photographs | people/objects/actions, location language, Present Continuous, passive recognition | literal descriptions with obvious contrasts | precise action/state/location and plausible distractors | concise visual inference boundaries, speed, rare vocabulary control |
| Part 2 Question–Response | question words, auxiliaries, intent, basic short responses | direct questions and answers | indirect responses, requests/offers, sound and keyword traps | subtle intent, elliptical responses, rapid switching |
| Part 3 Conversations | turn-taking, roles, common situations, detail tracking | short two-speaker exchanges | longer/two- or three-speaker sets, purpose/inference, graphics | authentic rate, dense paraphrase, implied action, timing |
| Part 4 Talks | announcements, sequence, numbers, purpose | short predictable talks | varied talk types, organization, inference, graphics | dense delivery, subtle purpose, embedded detail, timing |
| Part 5 Incomplete Sentences | sentence structure, parts of speech, core grammar/lexis | one clearly taught contrast | mixed form/meaning/collocation decisions | rapid full-sentence analysis, nuance, exception control |
| Part 6 Text Completion | paragraph meaning, cohesion, tense/reference | local grammar/word choices | discourse connectors and sentence insertion | whole-text coherence, register, speed |
| Part 7 Reading Comprehension | functional text types, scanning, evidence | short single texts | longer singles and double passages | triple passages, cross-document reasoning, inference, pacing |

The universal sequence is:

**concept learning → TOEIC-style example → isolated question → mixed untimed practice → short timed set → timed part/section → realistic mock exam**

Learners may move at different rates by part. Full mocks begin only when format coverage, stamina, and diagnostic value justify them.

## 13. Assessment milestones

### Entry baseline

Establish what the learner can actually do without treating an unknown-format TOEIC test as the sole beginner diagnostic. Sample print awareness, sound recognition, core vocabulary, sentence comprehension, and study access.

### Lesson and weekly evidence

Lesson checks guide immediate feedback. Weekly or learning-cycle reviews mix recent and older material. These do not independently determine phase movement.

### Periodic cumulative reviews

After coherent curriculum blocks, sample durable retrieval, transfer, interleaving, and key skill trends. The interval is based on content density and evidence, not a rigid calendar.

### Phase assessments

Each phase assessment includes:

- prerequisite knowledge;
- untimed or lightly timed language competence;
- listening and reading comprehension;
- pronunciation/perception where foundational;
- vocabulary and grammar in context;
- independent application;
- TOEIC-format evidence appropriate to the phase;
- an error profile and remediation decision.

### TOEIC simulations

Use adapted mini-tests first, then part clusters, timed sections, and full-length simulations. Record form, date, audio conditions, timing, raw/subscores or scaled estimate method, confidence, and error categories. Never combine incomparable conditions into a false trend.

## 14. Transition decision rules

Phase transition is a portfolio decision, not a single cutoff.

- **Strong mastery:** prerequisites are accurate, independently accessible, durable after spacing, and transferable. Progress normally; schedule maintenance review.
- **Partial mastery:** core meaning is sound but some speed, form, or contexts remain weak. Progress where dependencies allow and maintain targeted review.
- **Weak mastery:** the learner repeatedly misunderstands a concept or cannot apply it even with reasonable support. Remediate before advanced work depends on it.

A phase exit requires:

1. no severe unresolved gap in a prerequisite that the next phase relies on heavily;
2. sufficient performance in each major track, interpreted by skill-appropriate criteria;
3. more than one observation for consequential decisions;
4. evidence after delay, not only immediate post-teaching success;
5. an explicit plan for partial but non-blocking weaknesses;
6. learner ability to operate at the next phase's expected independence.

Listening, reading, grammar, vocabulary, and foundational perception are not averaged blindly. For example, strong reading must not conceal listening that is too weak for the next phase.

## 15. Difficulty-control matrix

Curriculum planners increase difficulty deliberately across independent dimensions:

- vocabulary frequency, abstraction, polysemy, and collocational precision;
- sentence and clause complexity;
- listening rate, reductions, accent variety, and speaker count;
- passage length, information density, genre variety, and source count;
- directness versus paraphrase and implication;
- distractor plausibility;
- memory span and cross-reference distance;
- time pressure;
- independence from hints, translations, transcripts, and topic labels.

Only a limited number of dimensions should jump at once unless the activity is a deliberate challenge or readiness assessment. Obscure vocabulary alone is not legitimate difficulty design.

# Daily TOEIC Journey — Knowledge Dependency Architecture

## 1. Purpose and notation

This document defines the high-level prerequisite architecture used to order curriculum. It is not a list of daily lessons and not a claim that language develops in one perfectly linear sequence. Planners may spiral back, preview future concepts, and develop parallel tracks, but they must not require mastery of a concept before its essential prerequisites are established.

Notation:

- `A → B`: A is a normal prerequisite for B.
- `A + B → C`: both A and B are normally required.
- `[R]`: primarily receptive mastery at first.
- `[P]`: controlled productive use is also expected.
- dotted or cross-track relationships described in tables are enabling links rather than strict linear gates.

Each curriculum concept should eventually receive a stable ID and reference these high-level nodes. A prerequisite can be waived only when diagnostic evidence already establishes it; the decision must be recorded.

## 2. Whole-course dependency map

```text
Learning conventions + print awareness + sound awareness
    ├──> Word recognition + core vocabulary
    ├──> Basic pronunciation perception
    └──> Word / phrase / sentence distinction
              └──> Subject + verb + basic word order
                        ├──> Clause and sentence grammar
                        │        └──> Complex clauses and discourse
                        ├──> Sentence-level listening
                        │        └──> conversations / talks / inference
                        └──> Sentence-level reading
                                 └──> paragraphs / documents / multi-source reasoning

Vocabulary breadth + grammatical parsing + sound/print access
    └──> Meaning comprehension
             └──> paraphrase recognition + reference tracking
                      └──> inference + distractor evaluation
                               └──> timed TOEIC Parts 1–7 performance

Durable knowledge + strategy control + stamina
    └──> section performance
             └──> realistic full-test performance
                      └──> high-score consistency and C1-oriented autonomy
```

General English nodes feed TOEIC nodes. TOEIC format knowledge never substitutes for vocabulary, grammar, listening perception, or reading comprehension.

## 3. English foundations graph

```text
F0 Learning navigation
   ├── audio controls / replay conventions
   ├── transcript and answer-reveal rules
   ├── exercise symbols and response methods
   └── mistake and review routines

F1 Print and sound orientation
   ├── Roman alphabet recognition where needed
   ├── uppercase / lowercase
   ├── letter name versus speech sound
   ├── spaces and word boundaries
   └── basic punctuation
          ↓
F2 Units of language
   word → phrase → clause → sentence
          ↓
F3 Functional word classes
   naming words (nouns)
   person/thing substitutes (pronouns)
   action/state words (verbs)
   describing words (adjectives/adverbs)
   relationship/linking words (prepositions/conjunctions)
          ↓
F4 Sentence core
   subject + verb
      ├── subject + be + complement
      ├── subject + intransitive verb
      ├── subject + transitive verb + object
      ├── subject + linking verb + complement
      └── subject + verb + indirect/direct objects [later]
          ↓
F5 Sentence operations
   agreement + verb form awareness
      ├── statements
      ├── negation
      ├── yes/no questions
      ├── wh- questions
      └── commands
          ↓
F6 Expansion and connection
   modifiers + coordination
      └── clause boundaries + subordination
             └── complex sentences + discourse relationships
```

### Foundation decisions

- The learner first meets plain-language functions; technical labels are attached only after the function is understandable.
- `Clause` depends on recognizing a subject–verb relationship. It should not be defined as merely “a group of words.”
- Parts of speech and sentence roles are distinct. For example, a noun is a word class; subject is a sentence role. This distinction is introduced gradually but never taught incorrectly.
- Punctuation supports boundary detection and reading prosody; it is linked to, but does not replace, grammar.

## 4. Grammar dependency graph

### 4.1 Noun phrase system

```text
Concrete noun meaning
   → singular versus plural
      → count versus noncount
         → articles a/an/the/zero
         → demonstratives and possessives
         → quantifiers and amount expressions
            → determiner compatibility and specificity
               → complex noun phrases
                  → prepositional / participial / relative modification
                     → dense nominalization and advanced modification

Personal reference
   → subject pronouns
      → object pronouns
         → possessive adjectives / pronouns
            → reflexive and reciprocal forms
               → reference tracking across clauses and texts
```

Important order decisions:

- Plural marking begins before the full countability system, but article choice is revisited after countability is taught.
- `a/an`, `the`, and zero article are not taught as a one-lesson closed system; definiteness, shared knowledge, generic reference, and institutional uses spiral across phases.
- Dense noun phrases depend on reliable head-noun identification, word classes, and modifier boundaries.

### 4.2 Verb phrase, agreement, tense, and aspect

```text
Action/state meaning + subject recognition
   → be / lexical verb distinction
      → base form and finite verb awareness
         → present agreement (-s)
         → do-support for questions/negatives
         → common irregular forms
            ↓
Time reference
   → Present Simple
   → Present Continuous
      → present simple/continuous contrast
   → Past Simple
      → past versus present
   → future meanings (will / going to / scheduled arrangements)
      → time-form choice by meaning, not one-form-per-time shortcuts
            ↓
have + past participle
   → Present Perfect
      + Past Simple
         → present perfect/past simple contrast
         → Past Perfect
         → Future Perfect [later, receptive priority]

be + -ing + simple/progressive control
   → past/future progressive
      → perfect progressive meanings

tense/aspect network + discourse time cues
   → sequence of events, background/foreground, current relevance
      → advanced temporal interpretation in conversations and documents
```

Important order decisions:

- Learners need verb-form awareness before a complete tense taxonomy.
- Time words are clues, not automatic answer machines. Form, meaning, and discourse context must agree.
- The past participle is introduced as a verb form before it supports perfect aspect, passive voice, and reduced clauses.
- Rare forms may begin as receptive knowledge and deepen only when useful.

### 4.3 Auxiliaries, questions, negation, and modality

```text
be as a finite verb
   → be questions / negatives

lexical verbs + base form
   → do/does questions and negatives
      → did questions and negatives

question words + statement structure
   → wh- questions
      → subject versus object questions
         → embedded questions / noun-clause word order

base verb + basic intent
   → can / cannot
      → permission, ability, requests
         → should / must / have to
            → may / might / could / would
               → modality of probability, obligation, willingness, advice, politeness
                  → perfect modals and advanced stance [R before P]
```

Question form precedes advanced Part 2 intent analysis. Modal form precedes nuanced indirect requests and inference about certainty.

### 4.4 Complements and non-finite forms

```text
main verb + object/complement recognition
   → to-infinitive for purpose
   → selected verb + to-infinitive
   → selected verb/preposition + gerund
   → participles as adjective-like forms
      ↓
verb complementation patterns
   ├── verb + object + to-infinitive
   ├── bare infinitive after modals/selected verbs
   ├── gerund versus infinitive with meaning differences
   └── perception/causative patterns
      ↓
non-finite clauses
   → infinitive clauses
   → participial clauses
   → reduced relative/adverb clauses
```

The label *gerund* is introduced only after `-ing` forms are seen in clear functions. Learners must not be taught that every `-ing` word is the same structure.

### 4.5 Modification and comparison

```text
noun + basic adjective
   → adjective position and linking-verb complement
      → adjective phrase / complement

verb/adjective + basic adverb
   → manner, frequency, degree, sentence adverbs
      → adverb position and scope

adjective/adverb distinction
   → word-family form selection
      → modifier attachment and ambiguity

gradable adjectives + quantity comparison
   → comparative / superlative
      → equality and less/fewer
         → intensified and proportional comparison
            → complex comparative structures
```

Word-class selection is a prerequisite for many TOEIC Part 5 items, but it must be grounded in sentence role and meaning rather than suffix guessing alone.

### 4.6 Voice and causation

```text
subject / verb / object
   + be forms
   + past participle
      → present/past passive
         → passive across tenses
         → passive with modals
         → by-agent and agent omission
            → get-passive / reporting passive [R]
            → causative have/get
               → passive infinitive/gerund and reduced passive modification
```

Active/passive paraphrase requires preservation of participant roles, time, and focus. Passive voice is not taught as mere word rearrangement.

### 4.7 Clause combination

```text
complete simple clauses
   → coordination (and / but / or / so)
      → adverb-clause markers
         ├── time
         ├── reason/result
         ├── condition
         ├── contrast/concession
         └── purpose
      → noun clauses
         ├── that-clauses
         ├── whether/if clauses
         └── embedded wh- clauses
      → relative clauses
         ├── people/things/places/time
         ├── subject/object relatives
         ├── defining/non-defining
         └── omission/preposition patterns
            ↓
multi-clause parsing
   → reported speech
   → conditional systems
   → reduced clauses
   → embedded and compressed advanced sentences
   → discourse-level cohesion and information structure
```

### 4.8 Conditionals and reported speech

```text
present/future time + if-clause boundaries
   → real/likely conditions (zero/first patterns)
      + past-form hypothetical meaning
         → second conditional
            + past perfect
               → third conditional
                  → mixed conditions and implied conditions [advanced]

statement/question forms + reporting verbs
   → reported statements
      → reported questions/commands
         → tense/time/pronoun perspective shifts
            → stance and evidential reporting
```

Numbered conditional labels are secondary to time, reality, and speaker perspective. Reported speech depends on question word order and reference control.

### 4.9 Advanced grammar and discourse

```text
complex clauses + modification + reference
   → reduced relative/adverb clauses
   → nominalization and dense noun phrases
   → ellipsis and substitution
   → inversion and emphasis [mainly R]
   → modality and stance
   → information structure / focus
   → cohesive devices and rhetorical relations
   → ambiguity resolution and long-distance dependency
```

These nodes support B2/C1 comprehension. Productive mastery is assigned selectively according to course objectives; receptive parsing is essential.

## 5. Vocabulary dependency architecture

Vocabulary has dependencies of breadth, depth, morphology, and domain—not just word-count order.

```text
V0 Learning words
   form + sound + contextual meaning + basic retrieval
      ↓
V1 High-frequency foundation
   function words + people + objects + core actions + qualities + numbers/time
      ↓
V2 Everyday semantic networks
   home + routines + food + places + transport + health + services
      ↓
V3 Functional communication
   requests + schedules + problems + transactions + travel + messages
      ↓
V4 Workplace core
   roles + departments + office + meetings + documents + communication
      ↓
V5 TOEIC domain expansion
   HR / training / purchasing / finance / sales / marketing / retail
   manufacturing / logistics / shipping / travel / hospitality / events
   healthcare / technology / construction / real estate / insurance
   maintenance / facilities / announcements / policies
      ↓
V6 Cross-domain precision
   collocation + polysemy + word family + register + phrasal/prepositional patterns
      ↓
V7 Advanced general/professional/selected academic vocabulary
   abstraction + nuance + connotation + nominalization + discourse vocabulary
```

### Lexical-depth dependencies

```text
recognize written form
   ↔ recognize spoken form
      → retrieve core contextual meaning
         → distinguish senses
            → recognize common collocations
               → recognize word family / grammar behavior
                  → recognize synonyms and paraphrases
                     → understand register and nuance
                        → use independently where required
```

The arrows show a common progression, not a universal sequence. Spoken and written recognition should reinforce each other early. Productive use is not required for every low-frequency receptive word.

### Morphology and paraphrase links

```text
part-of-speech awareness
   + common affixes
      → word-family recognition
         → form choice in sentences
         → nominalization / compressed business style

stable core meaning
   → direct synonym
      → phrase substitution
         → grammatical paraphrase
            → contextual/indirect paraphrase
               → implication with meaning constraints
```

Domain vocabulary should be recycled across genres. For example, `shipment` should appear in an order, an email, a tracking notice, a conversation, and a delay announcement rather than remaining in one topical list.

## 6. Pronunciation dependency graph

```text
P0 Sound awareness
   speech sound ≠ letter name; mouth/voice awareness; listen before imitate
      ↓
P1 Segment perception
   high-impact vowels and consonants
      ├── initial consonants
      ├── final consonants
      └── selected Vietnamese-learner contrasts
      ↓
P2 Word shape
   syllables + consonant clusters + final grammatical endings
      → plural / third-person / past ending perception
      → word stress and reduced unstressed vowels
      ↓
P3 Phrase prosody
   content/function words + sentence stress + rhythm + thought groups
      ↓
P4 Connected speech
   linking + contractions + weak forms + common assimilation/deletion patterns
      ↓
P5 Meaning through prosody
   focus + contrast + attitude + question/statement contours
      ↓
P6 Variation and automatic perception
   speaker/accent/rate accommodation + ambiguous boundary resolution
```

### Key decisions for Vietnamese learners

- Final consonant perception begins early because it carries lexical and grammatical information.
- Clusters are developed after individual consonant awareness but revisited with real words and endings.
- Word stress precedes systematic weak-form and rhythm work.
- IPA symbols are introduced only as useful labels after the relevant sounds are perceptually grounded.
- Production drills serve perception and intelligibility; accent conformity is not a gate.

## 7. Listening dependency graph

```text
audio navigation + attention routine
   → sound discrimination
      → spoken-word recognition
         → phrase chunking / boundary detection
            → sentence meaning and grammatical cues
               ↓
L1 Literal comprehension
   names / spelling / numbers / dates / times / locations / actions
      → short question intent and response
      → gist and specific detail
               ↓
L2 Discourse comprehension
   speaker roles + setting + purpose
      → sequence + problem/solution + next action
      → reference across turns
               ↓
L3 Connected input
   reductions + linking + natural rate + accent variation
      → multi-turn/two-speaker comprehension
      → talks and announcements
      → three-speaker tracking
               ↓
L4 Indirect meaning
   lexical/grammatical paraphrase
      → intention + attitude
      → supported inference
      → distractor rejection
               ↓
L5 Integrated performance
   graphic reference + selective notes + memory control
      → timed set processing
      → high-speed multi-speaker and dense-talk comprehension
```

### Enabling relationships

| Listening skill | Required support |
|---|---|
| Hear plural/tense details | final consonants, grammatical endings, noun/verb knowledge |
| Understand Part 2 intent | question forms, modals, speech acts, elliptical responses |
| Track roles | person/job vocabulary, pronouns, turn-taking cues |
| Recognize paraphrase | lexical networks plus grammar transformations |
| Infer next action | explicit-detail comprehension plus purpose/intent reasoning |
| Survive natural rate | automatic core vocabulary, chunking, reductions, attention control |
| Use notes effectively | gist/detail distinction; notes must not compete with listening |

Dictation depends on sound recognition, segmentation, spelling conventions, and target vocabulary. It is a diagnostic/learning tool, not a universal prerequisite for all comprehension.

## 8. Reading dependency graph

```text
R0 Print/word recognition
   → phrase chunking
      → sentence core (subject + finite verb)
         → objects/complements/modifiers
            → clause boundaries and connector meaning
               ↓
R1 Sentence comprehension
   reference words + time/logic + vocabulary in context
      → simple paragraph topic and support
               ↓
R2 Functional texts
   notice / message / form / schedule / advertisement / email
      → scanning for explicit details
      → purpose and intended reader
               ↓
R3 Extended texts
   paragraph organization + cohesion + main idea
      → inference + tone + writer purpose
      → vocabulary from context
               ↓
R4 Multi-document reading
   document genre + source/role + reference tracking
      → double-passage connection
      → chronology / update / contradiction
      → triple-passage synthesis
               ↓
R5 Advanced reading
   dense noun phrases + compressed clauses + discourse relations
      → subtle stance and implication
      → rapid evidence verification
      → unfamiliar-domain and C1-oriented comprehension
```

### Strategy prerequisites

- **Scanning** requires knowing what information form to seek (name, date, action, amount) and enough genre knowledge to predict its location.
- **Skimming** requires sentence/paragraph topic recognition; it is not random fast reading.
- **Inference** requires accurate literal evidence plus a defensible bridge. It must not be taught as guessing.
- **Vocabulary in context** requires syntax, surrounding meaning, word-part awareness, and checking whether the inferred sense fits.
- **Cross-document reasoning** requires independent comprehension of each source before synthesis.
- **Speed work** follows accurate processing. Pushing speed on a broken parsing strategy rehearses error.

### TOEIC dependency decisions

- Part 5 does not precede basic sentence understanding merely because its items are short.
- Part 6 requires discourse cohesion, not only a collection of Part 5 rules.
- Part 7 multi-source work begins only after the learner can understand each component genre.
- Part 2 indirect responses depend on speech-act understanding, not keyword matching.
- Parts 3/4 inference follows reliable explicit-detail comprehension.
- Timing is added after format and core processing are sufficiently accurate to produce useful diagnostic evidence.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Understand `works` vs `worked` | word/verb awareness | agreement and tense | verb meaning | final endings | sentence context |

This matrix prevents planners from assigning a failure to “strategy” when it is actually caused by language or perception.

## 11. Spiral and preview rules

Dependencies do not require one-and-done teaching. Use three passes where appropriate:

1. **Preview/awareness:** identify a future pattern and provide just enough meaning to avoid confusion; do not assess mastery.
2. **Core instruction:** teach the concept at the current phase's required depth and assess it.
3. **Expansion/consolidation:** add exceptions, nuance, speed, genres, or advanced forms after the base is durable.

Examples:

- Relative clauses may first be recognized in simple descriptions, then taught systematically, then reduced at B1+/B2.
- Present Perfect may first express experience/result, later contrast with Past Simple and Past Perfect, then support discourse time and advanced passives.
- Weak forms may begin with common contractions, expand to function words and linking, then support accent/rate accommodation.

A preview must be labeled **Future concept** and cannot become an unexplained prerequisite.

## 12. Dependency validation rules for planners

Before placing a concept in curriculum:

1. identify its node and intended depth;
2. list strict and enabling prerequisites;
3. confirm the prior curriculum teaches them and current learner state supports them;
4. identify cross-track load, including incidental vocabulary and listening/reading demands;
5. decide whether gaps require a refresher, remediation, or later placement;
6. connect the concept to review and future dependents;
7. ensure any preview is non-mastery and clearly labeled;
8. record the decision in the curriculum plan.

An advanced TOEIC frequency claim cannot override this validation. If a common test feature is too advanced, planners may simplify the context, teach enabling knowledge, or defer full application.

## 13. High-risk prerequisite hubs

The following hubs deserve conservative mastery checks because many later capabilities depend on them:

- subject–finite verb identification;
- English word order and clause boundaries;
- noun countability, number, and determiner basics;
- base/past/past-participle/`-ing` form recognition;
- auxiliaries in questions and negation;
- core tense/aspect meanings;
- modifier attachment and part-of-speech function;
- high-frequency function words and core vocabulary;
- final consonant and grammatical-ending perception;
- word stress, chunking, and common reductions;
- reference tracking and logical connectors;
- evidence-based paraphrase and inference.

Weakness at a hub should trigger targeted repair before several downstream lessons accumulate unstable knowledge.

## 14. What this document does not own

This file does not store daily order, lesson text, individual learner mastery, error history, review dates, or test scores. `curriculum/` will instantiate these dependencies into sequences; `state/` will record learner evidence; `LEARNING_SYSTEM.md` will determine review and remediation actions.
